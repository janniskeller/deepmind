Jannis Keller , Application for Program Manager, Office of the CEO

“The OCEO’s primary objective is to maximise the signal-to-noise ratio in both directions. Top-down, only the strategic priorities that teams can act on should enter the system. Bottom-up, only what requires executive decision should reach the CEO’s desk. This requires three things: a common strategic language, leaders and processes enabled to enforce it and a synthesis layer that gets sharper with every cycle.”

Proposed Operational Architecture

01. Establishing the Strategic Taxonomy

The largest source of noise in any CEO office is language fragmentation: every team reports in its own vocabulary, its own metrics, its own definition of success. The OCEO is forced to act as translator, which is pure overhead. A unified strategic taxonomy that operationalizes the CEO’s high-level strategy into a shared framework (top-down flow) and a clear defined threshold for executive action (bottom-up flow) should be established. Every piece of information entering the OCEO must explicitly map to an active strategic priority using this common language. If an update cannot be tagged against the taxonomy, it is flagged as operational noise and does not reach the CEO’s desk. The standardised templates called for in this role are the vehicles for enforcing this taxonomy at the point of entry.

Methodology: Organizational Design & Strategy Translation

02. Implementation: People, Processes & Tools

A taxonomy on paper changes nothing. The real work is getting leaders across the organization to adopt it. This requires three levers simultaneously. First, coaching and leadership development: working with senior stakeholders and their teams to self-filter, to think in strategic altitude before they escalate and to use the common language as second nature. Second, process design: standardised reporting cadences, data-backed agenda criteria for leadership forums and documented governance structures that turn the taxonomy into operating rhythm. Forums become decisive alignment environments, not update sessions. Third, tooling: the right collaboration and reporting infrastructure to make compliance easy, not burdensome. Generally the new structure should not create overhead, so it needs to stay simple and must be easy to adopt.

Methodology: Change Management, Organizational Design & Leadership Development

03. AI-Augmented Synthesis & Feedback Loops

Once you have the language, the escalation thresholds and leadership buy-in, you still face the sheer volume problem. Instead of the CEO reading ten status reports to find one underlying issue, AI tooling aggregates and synthesises raw data from partner teams into high-signal briefs: here is the trend, here is the risk, here is the recommended decision. But synthesis alone is not enough, the system must learn. Closed-loop feedback mechanisms track what leadership actually acts on versus what gets ignored, using that data to continuously tighten the filter and surface blind spots. The ecosystem becomes a self-correcting product, not a static governance document, one that gets sharper with every cycle.

Methodology: AI Leverage & Continuous Improvement
